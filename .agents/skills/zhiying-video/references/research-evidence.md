# Research and Evidence

Use this reference when defining the research question, investigating sources, assessing factual claims, or closing evidence gaps.

## Frame the inquiry

Turn a broad topic into one researchable core question. It should be open to disconfirmation and should not hide the desired conclusion inside the wording. Split it into subquestions that cover:

- the facts the argument depends on;
- competing explanations or critical positions;
- definitions whose ambiguity could change the conclusion;
- factual uncertainties that still require source work.

Plan searches around those uncertainties, using both subject vocabulary and likely primary-source terminology. Research should include adverse or critical evidence, not only support for the intended position.

## Classify what is being said

Keep these distinct:

- **Factual claim:** externally checkable history, number, event, quotation, or experimental result.
- **Theory:** what a named thinker or framework proposes; it is not automatically a fact.
- **Interpretation:** a reasoned reading of facts or theory.
- **Argument:** the video's conclusion drawn within stated boundaries.
- **Illustrative example:** clarifies an idea but cannot establish a general claim.

Do not turn correlation into proof, behavior into a hidden motive, one case into a population claim, or a theory into a modern scientific result.

## Evidence rules

- Give each important factual claim traceable support before it enters the script.
- Prefer primary sources for original claims and authoritative syntheses for field consensus; use modern research for what its study design actually supports.
- Preserve the source's scope, population, date, uncertainty, and wording strength.
- Label unverified leads and unsupported claims as gaps. Never invent a paper, author, URL, page, quotation, or measurement.
- Do not cite a secondary page as if it were the underlying study (source laundering).
- Avoid unsupported precision. If the source supports only an approximate or qualified statement, the script must remain approximate or qualified.
- A controversial central claim should not rest on one weak source. Seek independent support and credible counterarguments.
- Model knowledge can suggest what to verify, but is not itself evidence.

Record what each source supports, the maximum safe wording, and the inference it does not support. If reliable access to a cited source is unavailable, say so and keep the claim out of final factual prose.

## Completion test

Research is complete when the major argument's factual dependencies have adequate support, meaningful counterpositions have been considered, and remaining unknowns cannot change the core content direction. The volume of search results is not a completion criterion.

If a major unsupported claim remains, narrow or qualify the argument, obtain better evidence, or stop content progression. Do not conceal the gap in fluent prose.
