# Writing and Narration

Use this reference for argument structure, script development, spoken-language editing, or duration correction.

## Build the argument

Define the audience's likely starting belief and the change in understanding the video should earn. Organize a clear path through:

- an opening that enters the real question quickly;
- a bounded core claim;
- support whose function is explicit;
- examples that illustrate rather than over-prove;
- a genuine rebuttal for disputed claims;
- the scientific or factual boundary;
- a restrained ending that does not diagnose the viewer or overstate certainty.

Structure precedes phrasing. Do not write a striking conclusion and search for evidence afterward. Every factual passage must remain traceable to its evidence, and contrary evidence must not be hidden.

## Draft for logic, then speech

The logical draft should cover the argument without encyclopedia-style accumulation or repetition. Each passage needs one job: pose, explain, support, challenge, qualify, or conclude. Evidence and interpretation should be visibly related rather than placed beside each other without reasoning.

The spoken draft may compress and naturalize the logical draft, but it must not add facts, change the argument, replace examples, remove rebuttals, or strengthen the evidence wording.

For duration, estimate Chinese narration at roughly 240–280 characters per minute, then confirm against the real audio rather than treating the estimate as timing truth. Allocate time by argumentative importance, not equal chapter lengths.

## Make narration speakable

- Prefer short sentences with one main information unit.
- Use punctuation to create natural phrasing and intentional pauses.
- Replace dense written transitions with direct spoken movement.
- Expand or rewrite symbols, abbreviations, dates, names, and pronunciation-sensitive expressions when they may be read incorrectly.
- Keep the subject of theories explicit: say who proposes or interprets what.
- Do not read evidence annotations, visual directions, shot descriptions, or production metadata aloud.
- Let visuals carry suitable relationships or examples instead of narrating every displayed node and arrow.
- Preserve deliberate breathing room at real transitions; do not add decorative pauses everywhere.

## Writing review

Check that the opening reaches the question, the core claim is easy to state, every section advances it, evidence wording stays within support, and repeated explanations have been removed. Read the narration aloud or simulate a spoken pass to catch long clauses, tongue-twisters, ambiguous pronouns, and abrupt transitions.

If the duration is wrong, edit the lowest-value repetition or restore missing reasoning first. Do not solve a content-length problem by changing deterministic subtitle or frame timing.
